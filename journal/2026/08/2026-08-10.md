## Sprint Review: Day 1 → Day 4

### Executive Summary

The learner is developing a personal English voice. Output is becoming more complex, so accuracy has not yet caught up with expressive range. More errors now reflect ambitious communication rather than a lack of ideas.

### Learning System Findings

- Listening is the fastest-improving skill: the learner can now identify the main topic, benefits, and useful vocabulary. The next step is a 30-second paraphrase instead of only answering questions.
- Speaking now has an IELTS-friendly structure: what → why → how → benefit → overall. The main bottleneck is information density, not a lack of ideas.
- Writing is becoming more stable because the learner now outlines before drafting. Examples need one additional analysis sentence explaining why they matter.
- German is progressing at a healthy A1 pace through `sein` → `haben` → supported output without jumping into advanced grammar.

### Root Causes

1. The learner translates concepts instead of retrieving English chunks, producing logical but unnatural phrases such as `bring more benefit to the society`.
2. Fast production causes unfinished grammar such as `I really grateful.` Slow production is a higher-value intervention than more grammar drills.
3. Vocabulary retrieval is slower than vocabulary recognition. Repeated chunks such as `breathing techniques`, `maintain concentration`, `improve my performance`, and `make gradual progress` should be retrieved in speaking.
